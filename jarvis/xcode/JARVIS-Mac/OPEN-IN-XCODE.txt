Send your friend this folder, or the zip of it. He opens JARVIS.xcodeproj in Xcode. He does not paste code unless he already started his own Mac project.

This is a Mac app. It is not an iPhone app. Product, Archive uploads it to App Store Connect only after he signs it with his Apple Developer team.

Steps for him:

1. Install Xcode from the Mac App Store and join the Apple Developer Program. The membership is 99 USD per year.
2. Double-click JARVIS.xcodeproj.
3. Click the blue JARVIS project, then the JARVIS target, then Signing and Capabilities.
4. Choose his Team. Change the bundle id from com.jarvis.desktop to one he owns, such as com.hisname.jarvis. He also creates that id in his Apple developer account.
5. Menu Product, then Archive. When the archive window opens, choose Distribute App, then App Store Connect, then Upload.
6. In App Store Connect, open the macOS app record, wait until the build finishes processing, and add the description, screenshots, a privacy policy link, and the age rating. Then submit for review.

If he already has a macOS SwiftUI project, he can replace that project's ContentView.swift with JARVIS/ContentView.swift. The file has to stay in a Mac target. An iPhone target will not compile it.

What this build is: a sandboxed Mac window. It shows CPU load, memory, the Mac model, and the thermal state, then answers with a free plan and a paid plan. Fan speed, GPU temperature, and the Windows gaming-PC tuner are not in this file. Apple rejects those private sensors and a separate Node server.
